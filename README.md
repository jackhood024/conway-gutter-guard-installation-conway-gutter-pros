## Guard Types

Gutter guard types for Conway homes are the styles that cover an open gutter so water gets in and debris stays out. This Old House tested six: brush, foam insert, mesh screen, micro-mesh, PVC covers, and reverse-curve. Under pines, oaks, and sweetgums, the differences come down to what each one lets through.

- **Mesh screen:** stops medium-to-large debris. In This Old House's testing, screens "aren't great at blocking smaller debris, like pine needles or shingle grit."
- **Micro-mesh:** "highly effective at blocking debris of all sizes," though it is "difficult to install since they require precise fitting, especially on pitched or uneven roofs."
- **Reverse-curve:** directs rain into the gutter while shedding debris off the top, but these guards "don't handle large water quantities well, based on our testing."
- **Foam insert:** blocks larger debris, then wears out after a few seasons. It can trap debris on top and hold moisture that leads to mold or mildew.
- **Brush:** "one of the best guards for blocking pine needles, twigs, and leaves," but debris can mat in the bristles in wet regions.

Which type suits a given house is settled at the quote, because roof pitch and tree cover change from street to street.

## Upkeep After Installation

Gutter guard upkeep is the cleaning a guarded gutter still gets. This Old House says it plainly: "Gutter guards help reduce gutter maintenance, but they don't eliminate it entirely."

Its survey of guard owners found that "41% still clean them annually and roughly 22% clean them twice per year." Its suggested cleaning schedule by type:

- Micro-mesh: once or twice a year.
- Screen: 2 to 4 times a year.
- Brush: 3 to 4 times a year.
- Foam: 3 to 4 times a year.

Spring adds a load of its own. In This Old House's words, "Spring is also the season for pollen, seed pods, and early tree debris, which can collect on or beneath your guards." Homes surrounded by pine needles, acorns, or heavy leaf cover are far more likely to need cleaning twice a year or more.
